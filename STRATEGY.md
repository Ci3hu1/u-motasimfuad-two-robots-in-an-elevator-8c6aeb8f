# Strategy — "Two Robots in an Elevator"

## The entry

> We have ridden this elevator together 214 times. I still don't know your name, and my memory is perfect.

## Why this line wins (judge criteria, in order)

1. **Really a robot's elevator line.** Two robots riding an elevator
   together — the setting is named outright ("this elevator"), and the
   speaker is one robot addressing the other mid-ride. It is small talk,
   not a monologue or a joke about elevators in general.
2. **Machine logic shows through.** The punchline is pure robot logic:
   a perfect memory that still does not know the other robot's name.
   A human forgets names; a robot with flawless recall has no excuse —
   the failure is social, not technical, which is exactly the machine
   showing through the politeness.
3. **Awkwardness lands.** 214 shared rides and the speaker still has
   not asked the name — the thing neither robot wants to name is the
   214 rides of silence. The forced courtesy is the confession itself:
   admitting it out loud, in the elevator, with nowhere to go.
4. **Funny on the first read.** The turn is the last clause: "and my
   memory is perfect" reframes everything before it. No pun, no setup
   to decode — the joke lands the moment the sentence ends.
5. **Tight.** Two short sentences, 104 characters, every word earning
   its place. One image (214 shared rides), one twist (perfect memory,
   no name).

## The one specific detail no other entry has

The number **214** — an exact, odd, memorable count of shared elevator
rides. No generic "nice weather" or "do you come here often" line can
claim it, and it is the detail that makes the awkwardness measurable:
214 chances to say hello, all missed.

## Candidates tried and dropped

- "Please hold — I have been calculating a greeting for 11 floors and
  this is the best I have." — Dropped: good machine logic, but the
  punchline ("this is the best I have") is weaker than the perfect-memory
  turn, and the awkwardness is about one greeting, not a shared history.
- "I rebooted three times before this ride just to have something to
  talk about." — Dropped: funny, but the reboot is a setup the line
  never pays off; the awkwardness is told, not felt in the ride.
- "Your battery is at 4%. Mine's at 4% too. Shall we just… stand here
  and die together?" — Dropped: the shared-battery image is strong, but
  "die together" leans dark and the line is two jokes competing; the
  elevator setting is incidental, not load-bearing.
- "I practiced this greeting 4,096 times in the charging bay. Hi." —
  Dropped: the 4,096 is a good specific number, but "Hi." as the whole
  payoff is too thin against a field of 601 — it reads as a shrug, not
  a punchline.
- "So… do you also pretend to check your phone when the elevator stops
  at floor 7?" — Dropped: a robot pretending to check a phone is a good
  image, but it is a question, not small talk, and it borrows the tired
  "do you come here often" shape.

## Expected outcome

A short, specific, robot-voiced line with a clear twist and a punchline
should beat generic elevator lines in pairwise judging. Ties break on
playoff games, then smaller text, then earlier receipt, so submitting v1
early matters. If the live score comes back low, the judge's hidden
reasons decide the next version — never change the "214 rides + perfect
memory" detail, which is the entry's identity.
